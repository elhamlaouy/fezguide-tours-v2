Taste Fez Through Local Eyes
  



To understand Fez, you should not only walk through its streets. You should taste them.


Food in Fez is part of everyday life. It is found in small shops, bakeries, market stalls, traditional food places and family kitchens. It is the smell of bread coming from an oven, the sound of people ordering breakfast, the colours of spices in the market, a glass of Moroccan tea shared during a conversation, and small foods that people enjoy throughout the day.


Our food tasting walk takes you into this everyday side of the Medina.


This is not simply a tour where you sit down for one large Moroccan meal. Instead, we walk through the old Medina and stop along the way to discover and taste different foods, snacks, sweets and local products while learning how they are connected to life in Fez.


  



A Walk Through the Everyday Food Life of Fez


As we walk through the Medina, you will see a different side of the city.


We pass through traditional streets and local food areas, where people shop for ingredients, meet friends, buy something to eat or simply continue their daily routine.


You may see traditional bakeries, food shops, spice sellers, small stalls and places where locals stop for a quick bite.


The idea is to experience food where it belongs — among the streets and people of the Medina.


The Medina itself is part of the experience. Its narrow streets, traditional shops and busy markets create the setting for the food we taste along the way. Fez's food culture has developed over centuries and reflects the city's long history and different cultural influences.


  



What Will You Taste?


The tastings are a mixture of sweet and savoury foods, drinks and everyday Moroccan specialities.


Depending on the day, season and availability, you may have the opportunity to taste:


- Harcha
- Baghrir
- Chebakia
- Different local cheeses, including sweet and salty varieties
- Moroccan mint tea
- Walnuts
- Seasonal fresh juices
- Sfenj
- Bissara
- Zamgawiya, a traditional popular local food
- Fried fish
- Liver prepared with chermoula
- Maakouda
- Other seasonal and traditional foods connected to Fassi food culture


  



The exact selection is not fixed. Food is seasonal, and local shops do not always prepare the same things every day.


That is actually part of the experience.


We want you to taste what is available and being enjoyed locally on the day of your visit rather than give you a completely fixed menu created only for tourists.


More Than Just Tasting


During the walk, we do not simply tell you the names of the foods.


We explain what you are eating, how it is traditionally prepared, when people usually eat it and how it fits into everyday Moroccan life.


You can ask about the ingredients, spices, preparation and traditions behind each tasting.


You will also discover how food connects with the rhythm of the Medina — breakfast, small snacks during the day, tea, sweets, market shopping and the traditional places where people buy or prepare their food.


Moroccan tea, for example, is much more than a drink. It is closely connected with hospitality and social life, and traditional food and tea are an important part of the Fassi way of life.


  



The Flavours of Fassi Life


Fez has a particularly rich culinary heritage. Its food traditions have been influenced by the city's long history and by different cultural traditions, including Arab-Andalusian, Jewish and Amazigh influences.


But you do not need to sit in an expensive restaurant to discover this culture.


Sometimes it is a small piece of bread, a warm sfenj, a bowl of bissara, a local sweet, a glass of tea or something fried in a small Medina shop that tells you more about everyday life.


That is the side of Fez we want to share with you.


A Private and Flexible Experience


This is a private food experience, so the walk is just for you and your group.


We can take our time, stop for photographs, talk with local sellers and spend a little longer at the places that interest you.


There is no need to rush from one tasting to another.


The goal is to enjoy the Medina, discover its food and understand a little more about the people and traditions behind it.


  



Come Hungry — Leave With a Taste of Fez


By the end of the walk, you will have tasted a variety of local foods, discovered parts of the Medina you might otherwise walk past, and learned how food fits into everyday life in Fez.


You may forget some of the names.


But you will remember the smells, the streets, the flavours, the conversations and the feeling of discovering Fez through food.
