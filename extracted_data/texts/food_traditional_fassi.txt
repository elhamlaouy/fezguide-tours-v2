Discover the Traditional Food of Fez


  



Food in Fez has its own character.


The city is known for a rich culinary tradition that has been passed from one generation to another. Many recipes are connected to family kitchens, celebrations, religious occasions and the traditions of Fassi households.


For us, traditional food is not simply about eating a famous Moroccan dish. It is about understanding where the food comes from, how it is prepared, when people eat it and why certain dishes have a special place in Fassi culture.


The Flavours of Fassi Cuisine


  



Fassi cuisine is known for its careful preparation, balanced flavours and the use of spices, herbs, dried fruits and other local ingredients.


Some dishes take time to prepare. Others are simple foods that have been part of everyday life for generations.


Among the traditional dishes you may discover are:


- Pastilla — one of the dishes strongly associated with Fez, traditionally combining thin pastry with a rich filling and a delicate balance of savoury and sweet flavours.
- Tajine — prepared in many different ways depending on the ingredients, season and family tradition.
- Harira — a traditional Moroccan soup enjoyed especially during Ramadan and also prepared in many homes throughout the year.
- Bissara — a simple and comforting dish made from dried beans or split peas and commonly enjoyed as an everyday meal.
- Maakouda — a popular potato-based food often enjoyed as a snack or part of a simple meal.
- Liver with chermoula — a traditional preparation combining liver with herbs, spices and Moroccan chermoula.
- Traditional breads and pastries — an important part of daily Moroccan food culture.
- Traditional sweets — including chebakia and other sweets prepared for celebrations and special occasions.


  



Pastilla and the Fassi Table


Pastilla deserves a special place when talking about Fez.


It is one of the dishes most closely associated with the city's culinary heritage. Traditionally prepared with very thin pastry and a carefully prepared filling, it represents the elaborate side of Fassi cooking.


The combination of different flavours and textures is part of what makes it distinctive.


But behind the dish is something even more interesting: the time, patience and technique involved in preparing it.


  



Food, Family and Celebrations


Traditional Fassi food is also connected to important moments in people's lives.


Family gatherings, weddings, religious celebrations and special occasions often bring particular dishes to the table.


Recipes can differ from one family to another. The same dish may be prepared slightly differently depending on the household, the occasion or the person cooking it.


This is one of the reasons we prefer to talk about Fassi food traditions rather than a single fixed menu.


There is no one recipe that represents every family in Fez.


From the Market to the Kitchen


  



To understand traditional food, you also need to understand the ingredients.


In the Medina, food culture begins in the markets.


Spices, fresh vegetables, olives, nuts, dried fruits, herbs, bread and other ingredients are bought according to what families need and what is available.


The traditional kitchen starts long before the food reaches the table.


It starts with choosing the ingredients, preparing them carefully and taking the time needed to cook them properly.
