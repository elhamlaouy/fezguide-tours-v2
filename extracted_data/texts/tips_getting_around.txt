Getting around Fez is different from moving around a modern city. The Old Medina was built around walking, with narrow streets, small passages and many pedestrian areas. Outside the Medina, taxis and cars are much more practical.
As a visitor, you have two main ways to explore the Old Medina: you can discover it with a local guide, or explore it independently. Both are possible, but each offers a different experience.
  

Walking in the Old Medina
Walking is the main way people move around the Old Medina.
Many streets and alleys are narrow and are designed mainly for pedestrians. As you walk, you pass homes, shops, traditional workshops, food stalls, souks and residential neighbourhoods.
For local people, this is simply part of everyday life. For visitors, it can feel like entering a completely different world.
The Medina is large, so there is no need to rush from one place to another. The streets themselves are part of the experience.
How Heavy Goods Move Through the Medina
The narrow streets of the Old Medina are not only used by people.
When heavy goods need to be transported through the small streets, people traditionally use carts, donkeys or mules to carry the load.
Many of the alleys are too narrow for normal vehicles, so the way goods are transported has adapted to the traditional structure of the Medina.
As you walk through the streets, you may see this happening as part of everyday life.
  

When Is the Medina Active?
The main commercial areas of the Old Medina generally become active from around 10:00 in the morning and start becoming quieter from around 20:00.
One important exception is the area around Bab Boujloud, where restaurants and other places can remain active much later, sometimes until around midnight.
The atmosphere of the Medina changes throughout the day. During the daytime, the streets are closely connected to shops, workshops, markets and everyday local life, while the Bab Boujloud area can remain lively into the evening.
Opening hours can vary depending on the business, day and season, so these times should be understood as a general rhythm rather than a fixed schedule.
  

Two Ways to Explore the Medina
As a visitor, you have two main choices: explore the Old Medina with a local guide or discover it on your own.
A local guide can make the experience easier by helping you understand what you are seeing, saving time, choosing practical routes and making navigation more comfortable.
Exploring independently is also possible, but the Old Medina can be confusing for first-time visitors. You may spend more time than expected going in the wrong direction or trying to find your way back to the main route.
If you choose to explore on your own, understanding a few local signs and simple routes can make a big difference.
How to Read the Signs in the Medina
There is a simple local navigation detail that can help visitors avoid unnecessary detours when walking through the narrow streets.
You may come across two different types of signs.
The first type is a square sign. It indicates that the alley has an entrance and an exit, or that it leads to an open public space.
The second type is a hexagonal sign. It indicates that the alley has an entrance but does not continue through. In other words, it is a dead end.
These small signs can be especially useful when exploring the Medina independently. Instead of entering an alley and discovering later that you have to turn back, you can check the sign first and understand whether the route continues or ends there.
  

  A Simple Route for Exploring the Medina on Your Own
If you want to discover a large part of Fez without a guide, there is a simple route that can help you organize your visit.
Start around the Mellah, where you can explore the Jewish Quarter and the area around the Royal Palace. From there, visit Jnan Sbil and then follow the main road towards Bab Boujloud.
Once you reach Bab Boujloud, enter the Old Medina.
From Bab Boujloud, you will find two important routes: Tala'a Sghira (Small Ascent) on the right and Tala'a Kebira (Great Ascent) on the left.
For first-time visitors who want to see many of the important places in the Medina, we recommend following Tala'a Kebira.
In our experience, a very large share of the Medina's important monuments, museums, souks and interesting places are located along or around this route.
You can follow the main route while also exploring some of the smaller side alleys when something catches your attention. The signs can help you understand whether an alley continues or ends.
  

Continue Towards Bab Rcif
As you continue along the route, you gradually move through the southern part of the Old Medina.
You can eventually reach the area of Bab Rcif.
By this point, you will have experienced a large part of the historic Medina and passed through many of its important areas.
Of course, you will not have seen everything. The Old Medina is far too large to discover completely in one visit. But this route gives independent travellers a practical way to experience a large part of the historic city without constantly wondering which direction to take.
From Bab Rcif, you can reach the nearest accessible road and take a petit taxi to continue to your hotel or next destination.
  

How to Leave the Old Medina
When you want to continue towards the New City, you can leave the pedestrian areas through one of the accessible gates or roads where vehicles can reach you.
There are several practical points around the Old Medina where you can find petit taxis, including areas around:
* Bab Boujloud
* Bab Rcif
* Bab Mahrouk
* Bab Ziat
* Ain Azliten
* Bab Guissa
* Batha
These points provide a practical connection between the pedestrian streets of the Old Medina and the wider road network of Fez.
  

Petit Taxis in Fez
The petit taxi is one of the easiest ways to move around Fez outside the pedestrian areas.
Fez's petit taxis are red, which makes them easy to recognize.
For short journeys from the Old Medina towards the New City, the fare is often around 10–20 MAD, depending on the distance and destination. This is a general local estimate rather than a fixed price for every journey.
A petit taxi can normally carry up to 3 passengers plus the driver.
If you are travelling with more than three people, you will usually need two taxis or another type of transportation.
A taxi can take you as close as possible to your destination by road. Once you reach a pedestrian area, you simply continue the rest of the way on foot.
  

Old Medina and New City
One of the most important things to understand about Fez is that the Old Medina and the New City are very different when it comes to transportation.
Inside the Old Medina, walking is essential because of the narrow streets and pedestrian areas.
Outside the Medina, taxis and cars are much more practical.
For this reason, getting around Fez often means combining both: walking through the historic areas and using a taxi or private car when moving between different parts of the city.
